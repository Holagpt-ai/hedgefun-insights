How Stocksist powers this calendar

The calendar is not a static list. Stocksist's data system ingests every event, the AI Analyst classifies and impact-scores it, and the hover cards, day briefs and alerts are all generated from that same feed.

1 · Ingest

Prices, earnings dates and options flow from Massive / Polygon; filings and IPOs from SEC EDGAR; catalysts from the news wires; CPI, PPI, FOMC and jobs from the macro calendar.

2 · Classify + score

Every event gets a type, a ticker and an AI impact score — High, Medium or Low. The score decides what shows in a day cell and what sits behind “+more”.

3 · Verify

Same rule as Significant Market Moves: the price move is verified separately from the catalyst, and every catalyst carries its source. Nothing is listed on a rumour alone.

4 · Brief + alert

The AI Analyst writes the morning day brief and the “why it matters” line on every event — and flags tomorrow's high-impact catalysts on your watchlist.

When data is missing — designed states

The live build must never show a blank chart or a fake read. These are the three states Lovable builds alongside the happy path.

Loading — chart

Skeleton chart while Massive / Polygon responds. If there's still no data after a few seconds, it becomes the error state below — never an empty box.

Empty — no AI read yet

AI read pending — levels unavailable. Most of the month's catalysts won't carry a full tape read. The event still lists (all events stay free and listed); the AI panel says this plainly instead of inventing levels.

Error — feed unavailable

A day cell whose feed failed reads “Catalyst feed unavailable — tap to retry” in place of its chips, so a broken feed never looks like a quiet day.

THE AI BOT — HOW IT MUST TALK (this is a hard spec, please judge us against it)
Beside the big chart sits an AI Analyst bot panel. It must talk like a trader, not like a generic sentiment widget — in the SAME voice on every surface (pinned card, pop-up, day view). The paragraph below is Tony's BEHAVIOR EXAMPLE: it specifies the pattern, it is not literal live copy, and its numbers are placeholders.

"SPCX is volatile now due to the recent Rocket launch Dragon. It is above VWAP — it previously closed at 137, currently 139. Sell wall at 140. It broke 140 at 12:05 pm but reversed back to 137 — too many sellers now, so I would wait to go long once it breaks 140 and shows support at that level."

The rules behind that voice:
1. Name the catalyst first — why the stock is moving.
2. State previous close vs current price vs VWAP.
3. Name the key levels: sell wall / resistance, support.
4. Narrate the intraday action with timestamps (broke 140 at 12:05 pm, reversed to 137).
5. Give a clear conditional stance — wait for the break AND support at the level; do not chase.
6. Plain trader language, specific numbers — all derived from one quote object per ticker. No bullish/neutral percentage fluff anywhere, on any surface.
7. Stance comes from the defined enum — WAIT / LONG BIAS / AVOID / NO TRADE — always with its trigger. Every read is stamped "as of [time] ET".
